How the Boltz-2 affinity prediction uses a structure

A Boltz-2 affinity run hands the affinity module two things that depend on the generated structures: the token set it scores, and a binned distogram.

Two structures are generated: stage 1 co-folds the complex, and stage 2 samples local poses again on the crop.

For the details, see the Boltz source code.

(a) The crop: which tokens the affinity checkpoint sees

The cropper keeps the ligand and the nearest receptor tokens, up to 256 tokens and 2,048 atoms. The checkpoint sees the pocket, not the whole receptor.

Token limit

The affinity checkpoint takes a fixed number of tokens, with limits on the input, the protein and the ligand.

Total256 tokens
Proteinup to 200 tokens
Ligand1 per heavy atom

The crop decides what the network conditions on, and Section 4 shows it moves the result.

(b) The distogram: how the local pose enters the score

The distogram is not built from the stage 1 complex. The affinity checkpoint samples local poses again on the crop (5 by default), keeps the one with the highest ipTM, and builds the distogram from it.

Structure checkpointStage 1 complexgives the crop (a)
Affinity checkpointLocal pose on the cropgives the distogram (b)

Making the distogram

Each token is one point. Every pairwise distance falls into one of 64 bins (2 to 22 Å), and each bin selects a learned embedding row.

Where it joins the affinity pathway

The embedded distogram meets the pair representation in the pairwise conditioner, whose output is added back to the pair representation.

03

What earlier work already suggested

Two independent readings point the same way: the pose the affinity module is handed matters much less than the context around it.

Boltzina[3] docks each ligand with Vina into a grid from one reference complex per target, then uses the released affinity pathway. Its study also rescored the top 5% to 50% with Boltz-2.

1New here

No per-compound structure generation or docking

Earlier two-stage designs need a docked pose or a full Boltz-2 pass per compound. GenFree generates none; any reference is prepared once per target.

2New here

Interventions on the retained inputs

Earlier tracing asked how far a pose moves the value. Here the question is which remaining inputs the ranking can lose.

3New here

Full libraries, not panels

Eight full libraries of about 50,000 compounds, against the released Boltz-2 and Boltzina scores.

04

Interventions: which inputs the ranking actually needs

Zeroing the distogram costs almost nothing, while the crop and the learned pair pathway around it are load-bearing.

Everything here runs on enriched 512-compound panels (128 positive, 384 negative), one input changed at a time. These runs isolate inputs; they do not measure screening performance, which Section 6 does on full libraries.

Embedded distogram

Ranking barely moves

Zeroing it leaves the ranking nearly unchanged.

 Input positions

3D detail not needed

Two-dimensional ligand positions do about as well as 3D conformers. Zeroed positions can cost AP.

 Receptor crop

No single direction

Swapping the crop helps on some targets and hurts on others.

 Forced cuts

Load-bearing

Interventions that destroy a path through the model generally collapse accuracy.

The condition study, A to E

Five routes differ in where the receptor crop and the distogram come from. B and C are diagnostics; D and E are the candidates.

Captured-context pathway interventions

Each compound's saved context is held fixed, which separates the distogram from the pair pathway it feeds. The four paths are the ones marked 4 in Section 2 (where the distogram joins the affinity pathway).

Single-input interventions

A second set changes one input at a time against a baseline: the reference-crop candidate (D) on GSK3A, GSK3B and NS5, the full-receptor candidate (E) on the other four. The numbers mark the input: 1 protein, 2 MSA, 3 ligand reference positions, 4 pairwise conditioning.

Crop-construction sensitivity

How the crop is built moves the result, and no construction tested here dominates. Raising the protein-token cap did not help on its own.

05

What GenFree replaces, and the two routes it offers

GenFree keeps the released affinity trunk and heads and replaces the two inputs that need a generated structure. Its two routes trade per-target preparation against token count.

Settings are checked, not optimised

MSA, pocket conditioning and recycling varied on 128-compound subsets of five targets. Pick a setting to see the routes on each target.

What the complete receptor costs

Five of the eight receptors exceed the 200-token ordinary limit. Where the reference crop comes from depends on the length and on what is on record.

06

Results on eight full libraries

On its own GenFree keeps 90% of the Boltz-2 mean AP, against 67% for Boltzina[3]. How much survives depends on the target and the endpoint.

The evidence is the eight MF-PCBA libraries of the Boltzina benchmark, about 50,000 compounds each, eight of the ten assays Boltz-2 held out. The context-supplied route is carried forward: higher AP on all eight targets than the context-free one.

Retention90%of the Boltz-2 mean AP kept by GenFree, context-supplied. Boltzina keeps 67%. Speed58 to 82×faster than a timed Boltz-2 run, and 12 to 36× faster than Boltzina, on 2,048 compounds. Continuous affinity0.726mean Pearson on four FEP+ series with a reference crop, against 0.729 for a generated-structure control. Side check, below.

Eight libraries, target by target

Against Boltzina the two endpoints tell the same story: context-supplied is ahead on GSK3B, ALR and GSK3A, behind on CTDSP1 and level elsewhere. Against Boltz-2 the answer changes with the endpoint. By AP one target is ahead and four are behind; by AUROC none is ahead and five are behind.

Speed: each workflow at its own defaults

GSK3B and NS5, the same 2,048 compounds, one GPU, one run per method. Recycling zero is GenFree’s own default.

GenFree is ahead of Boltzina here too. With 21 and 16 positives the subsets are small, so the full libraries stay the primary evidence.

Side check: continuous affinity on FEP+ series (optional)

Ranking a library and predicting pX in a congeneric series are different tasks. This check uses the four FEP+ series Boltz-2 reports, 87 compounds in all.

The two-stage screen: GenFree first, Boltz-2 on the top 10%

Re-ranking only the top 10% of the GenFree ranking with Boltz-2 reaches the eight-library mean AP of Boltz-2 alone, 0.0839 against 0.0840, at an estimated 8.5 to 8.9 times lower cost.

The cheap ranking can also choose which compounds to label

Fine-tuning the affinity modules on 300 GenFree-selected labels per target raises AP on 7 of the 8 targets, while AUROC rises on only 3.

The rest of the library

Does fine-tuning help?

On AP, mostly yes. AUROC tells a different story.

Does it matter who picks the 300 labels?

Feasible, not better. The usual labels need a Boltz-2 pass over the library. Here the GenFree ranking that screens it supplies them.

How does it compare with fine-tuning Boltz-2 itself?

Close mean AP, different compounds. Both are trained on the labels that the head-fine-tuning study used.

Selecting with GenFree and then fine-tuning ordinary Boltz-2 needs new Boltz-2 inference, and is left for future work.

Discussion

Summary

  1. 04Interventions

    The distogram can be zeroed at little cost. The crop and the pair pathway around it matter.

  2. 05Design

    GenFree is a cheap first stage: no structure is generated per compound. Its two routes trade per-target preparation against cost and accuracy.

  3. 06Results

    Alone, it keeps 90% of the Boltz-2 mean AP, against 67% for Boltzina.

    90%of Boltz-2 mean AP
  4. 07Two-stage screen

    With Boltz-2 on a short list, it recovers the library mean AP at 8.5 to 8.9 times lower cost.

    8.5–8.9×lower cost
  5. 08Label selection

    Its ranking can also pick the labels for fine-tuning.

Limits

Target
Accuracy varies widely by target, for Boltz-2 and Boltzina too.
Transfer
All results are on the eight benchmark libraries, chosen before the analysis. Transfer to other targets is untested.
Inherited
Boltz-2’s leakage control for these assays is protein-level, and its affinity training set is not public.
Labels
Training and label acquisition sit outside the runtime ratios.
Pose
GenFree gives no pose. A compound carried forward still needs a structure, at the scale of the short list.
Simulated
The two-stage scores and costs are projected from released scores, not run end to end.
Statistics
Counts and correlations are point estimates; intervals are exploratory.
