Ranking a library without building a structure for every candidate

Structure-generation-free ranking for first-stage affinity screening

At the same time, several studies suggest that the pose contributes little to the predicted affinity (Ujihara 2026; Furui and Ohue 2025).

We examined this in detail by intervening on the individual inputs of the affinity module.

Based on those results, we built GenFree. It uses the same Boltz-2 weights, generates no structure, and screens fast.

A Boltz-2 affinity run hands the affinity module two things that depend on the generated structures: the token set it scores, and a binned distogram.

Two structures are generated: stage 1 co-folds the complex, and stage 2 samples local poses again on the crop.

For the details, see the Boltz source code.

(a) The crop: which tokens the affinity checkpoint sees

The cropper keeps the ligand and the receptor tokens closest to it, up to 256 tokens (200 of them protein) and 2,048 atoms. The checkpoint sees the pocket, not the whole receptor.

Token limit

The affinity checkpoint takes a fixed number of tokens, with limits on the input, the protein and the ligand.

Total256 tokens
Proteinup to 200 tokens
Ligand1 per heavy atomOver 56: warning. Over 128: not supported.

The crop decides what the network conditions on, and Section 4 shows it moves the result.

(b) The distogram: how the local pose enters the score

The distogram is not built from the stage 1 complex. The affinity checkpoint samples local poses again on the crop (5 samples by default), keeps the one with the highest ipTM, and builds the distogram from its coordinates. The module contains no structure generation of its own beyond sampling local poses.

Structure checkpointStage 1 complexgives the crop (a)
Affinity checkpointLocal pose on the cropgives the distogram (b)

Making the distogram

Each token is one point. Every pairwise distance falls into one of 64 distance bins covering 2 to 22 Å, and each bin selects a row of a learned embedding.

Where it joins the affinity pathway

The embedded distogram meets the pair representation in the pairwise conditioner. The conditioner's output is added back to the pair representation, which continues to the affinity heads.

03

What earlier work already suggested

Two independent readings point the same way: the pose the affinity module is handed matters much less than the context around it.

Boltzina docks each ligand with Vina into a grid from one reference complex per target, then uses the released affinity pathway. Two-stage designs exist too: the Boltzina study rescored its top 5% to 50% with Boltz-2.

1New here

No per-compound structure generation or docking

Earlier two-stage designs need a docked pose or a full Boltz-2 pass for every compound first. GenFree generates no structure for any compound; a reference, where one is used, is prepared once per target.

2New here

Interventions on the retained inputs

Earlier tracing asked how far a pose moves the value. Here the question is which remaining inputs the ranking can lose.

3New here

Full libraries, not panels

Eight full libraries of about 50,000 compounds, against the released Boltz-2 and Boltzina scores.

04

Interventions: which inputs the ranking actually needs

Zeroing the distogram costs almost nothing, while the crop and the learned pair pathway around it are load-bearing.

Everything here runs on enriched 512-compound panels (128 positive, 384 negative) and on captured contexts, with one input changed at a time. The positive rate is set by design, so these runs isolate inputs; they do not measure screening performance, which Section 6 does on full libraries.

Embedded distogram

Ranking barely moves

Zeroing it leaves the ranking nearly unchanged.

 Input positions

3D detail not needed

Two-dimensional ligand positions do about as well as 3D conformers. Zeroed positions can cost AP.

 Receptor crop

No single direction

Swapping the crop helps on some targets and hurts on others.

 Forced cuts

Load-bearing

Interventions that destroy a path through the model generally collapse accuracy.

The condition study, A to E

Five routes differ in where the receptor crop and the affinity distogram come from. B and C are diagnostics that still read the generated complex; D and E are the candidates.

Captured-context pathway interventions

Each compound's saved context is held fixed and compared with an unmodified run on it. That separates the distogram from the pair pathway it feeds. The four paths are the ones marked 4 in Section 2 (where the distogram joins the affinity pathway).

Single-input interventions

A second set changes one input at a time against a baseline run in the same experiment: the reference-crop candidate (D) on GSK3A, GSK3B and NS5, the full-receptor candidate (E) on the other four. The numbers mark where each input enters the affinity computation: 1 protein, 2 MSA, 3 ligand reference positions, 4 pairwise conditioning.

Crop-construction sensitivity

How the crop is built moves the result, and no construction tested here dominates. Raising the protein-token cap did not help on its own, and the effect is not monotonic.

06

Results on eight full libraries

On its own GenFree keeps 90% of the Boltz-2 mean AP, against 67% for Boltzina. How much survives depends on the target and the endpoint.

The evidence is the eight MF-PCBA libraries of the Boltzina benchmark, about 50,000 compounds each, eight of the ten assays Boltz-2 held out. The context-supplied route is carried forward: higher AP on all eight targets than the context-free one.

Retention90%of the Boltz-2 mean AP kept by GenFree, context-supplied. Boltzina keeps 67%. Speed58 to 82×faster than a timed Boltz-2 run, and 12 to 36× faster than Boltzina, on 2,048 compounds. 07

The two-stage screen: GenFree first, Boltz-2 on the top 10%

Re-ranking only the top 10% of the GenFree ranking with the published Boltz-2 score reaches the eight-library mean AP of Boltz-2 alone, 0.0839 against 0.0840, at an estimated 8.5 to 8.9 times lower cost.

A first stage ranks the library. Its top share, fixed in advance at 5, 10 or 20%, is re-ordered by a published Boltz-2 score, and everything below keeps its first-stage order. The screen is simulated from released scores, with GenFree and Boltzina as first stages on the same libraries, shares and Boltz-2 score.

Shortlist share
the three shares analysed, fixed in advance; applies to every view

Averaged over the eight libraries the top-10% GenFree screen is level with Boltz-2 alone (difference −0.0001, interval includes zero), and GenFree alone is not. With Boltzina first the construction stays below Boltz-2 at every share, with intervals excluding zero. The GenFree reduction in cost is the larger at every share tested, 5 to 20%.

09

Limitations

What this evidence does not establish.

Accuracy varies widely by target True of every route here, Boltz-2 and Boltzina included. Results
Transfer is untested The eight targets were chosen before the analysis, and the panels that shaped the inputs come from the same libraries. Design
Inherited assumptions Boltz-2’s leakage control for these assays is protein-level, and its affinity training set is not public. Populations
Labels cost something Training and label acquisition sit outside the runtime ratios. Label selection
No pose GenFree reports no pose or binding mode. A compound carried forward still needs a structure built for it, at shortlist scale. Two-stage screen

Scores and cost

  • Published Boltz-2 and Boltzina scores are compound-ID-aligned references, not matched reruns.
  • The two-stage screen is simulated from released scores, not run end to end.
  • The NS5 Boltzina run returned 2,030 of 2,048 scores.

Reading the interventions

  • Mechanisms are hypotheses; the interventions are diagnostics inside a generated context.
  • Conditions B and C read the generated complex and stay diagnostics.
  • The token strip in Section 2 is schematic.

Statistics

  • Intervals are exploratory and unadjusted.
  • Counts and correlations are point estimates.

Fine-tuning

Trained
Both affinity modules, on detached contexts, trunk frozen.
Loss
Focal losses summed, α1 = 0.8, α2 = 0.6, γ = 2.
Optimizer
AdamW, learning rate 2 × 10−5, (β1, β2) = (0.9, 0.95), epsilon 10−8, no weight decay, clipping ten.
Schedule
Five epochs, batch one, accumulation four: 375 optimizer steps.
Draws
Learning seed zero; 1,500 draws per 300-record pool.
Precision
FP32 on detached BF16 contexts.
Metrics and intervals
Ranked score
The aggregate binary probability, the mean of the two affinity modules, ranked high to low.
Read with
AUROC, average precision, and enrichment at the top 1% and 5%.
Ties
AP is non-interpolated and groups tied scores; boundary ties are shared in proportion.
Intervals
Label-stratified paired compound bootstrap: 1,000 replicates for panel and seed contrasts, 2,000 for full-library and FT300 contrasts.
MW correction
Boltz-2’s own, 1.035 v − 0.600 MW0.3 + 2.833.

Install and usage

Python 3.10 or newer, installed from a tagged release. The affinity checkpoint downloads on first use.

git clone https://github.com/N283T/boltz-genfree.git
cd boltz-genfree
uv sync --frozen --extra cuda      # --extra cuda12 for CUDA 12 PyTorch builds
source .venv/bin/activate

A screen config takes one protein source and one ligand source.

version: 1

target:
  sequence: YOUR_PROTEIN_SEQUENCE
  msa: ./target.a3m          # or auto / empty
  crop:                      # optional when the complete receptor fits
    reference: ./reference-complex.cif
    reference_protein: A
    reference_ligand: L

ligands:
  csv: ./library.csv
  id_column: id
  smiles_column: smiles

output_dir: ./screen-output
boltz-genfree screen screen.yaml --prepare-only   # dry run: validate the inputs
boltz-genfree screen screen.yaml                  # run; scores are written to scores.csv

A re-run with the same inputs resumes. Options are documented in docs/usage.md and docs/finetuning.md.
